## Items and dimensions

An **item** is one thing in the collection: a unique, stable `id`, an optional `weight` (mark area
is proportional to it; the default is 1), and its category in each dimension, by id, in `values`.

A **dimension** is one way of grouping the items: an `id`, a `label` and its `categories` in the
order you want them drawn. A category can carry its own `color` and an `href`. Mark a dimension
`ordered: true` when its categories run in a meaningful order, such as decades or sizes.

A value that is `null`, missing, or not one of the dimension's categories is **unknown**. Unknown
items are drawn, last, in their own category, labelled by the dimension's `unknownLabel`.

## Encodings

One dimension **arranges** the marks: it orders them along the ridges, so its categories form runs
from the core outwards. **Colour**, **shape** and **lightness** can each show a dimension too, so one
print can show three things at once:

- leave `color` and `shape` out and they follow `arrange`; set one to `null` to turn it off;
- **lightness needs an ordered dimension**: the first category is faintest. On unordered categories,
  fainter and stronger would read as less and more, so that is an error;
- colour stays distinguishable to about 10 categories and the default shapes to 5; beyond those,
  `layout.warnings` says so;
- size is never an encoding: mark area is reserved for weight.

## Views, selection and callouts

A **view** is a named encoding. With several, `FingerprintViewSwitch` moves between them: marks
glide to their new places and the ridges stay put, because the print's form comes from all the data,
not the view.

A selection is a category _of a dimension_, so choosing "Unread" in the reading-state legend picks
out unread books whatever their genre. Choose it again to clear.

`annotations` pin short notes to the print. You write the words; the canvas points a leader at a real
mark of the categories you name, and drops a note whose categories have no mark. Callouts sit beside
the print in a wide container and are numbered beneath it in a narrow one.

## Without React

`layoutFingerprint({ items, dimensions, encoding })` is the whole layout as plain data, with no
React: positioned `nodes`, one of `legends` per encoded dimension, and the `ridges` as polylines, in
a fixed `width` × `height` frame. It throws `FingerprintDataError` on data it cannot draw truthfully;
`useFingerprint` returns that as `error` instead. See the
[API reference](/data-profile/reference/).

## The print's form

Fingerprints come in three patterns: **loop**, **whorl** and **arch**. `formFromItems`, which
`useFingerprint` uses, picks one from the data (an arch under 12 items, a loop when one category of
the first dimension holds 40% or more, otherwise a whorl) and places the core from the other
dimensions. It changes in coarse steps, so a collection keeps its print as it grows by an item or
two. Pass `form` to override any part of it.
